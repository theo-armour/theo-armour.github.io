# 2026-09-13 AI Refactor

When computer programmers use the term "refactor," it means they're going to try and:
- rewrite everything or part of something
- completely back from basic principles
- redesign something and start fresh
- fix lots of little things
- make it part of a bigger, better picture

Interestingly, sometimes a refactor works, and sometimes the refactor makes things worse. 

Sometimes the refactor clarifies things and takes charge of things. Other times, it means that the history of the project is lost, and the reasons why you did something get forgotten. 

As of the last few days, it seems that the leaders of the developers of AI are wanting to refactor what they're working on. 

There are several interesting things about this. 

Government officials and government bodies seem to have no idea what's going on. Let alone any ability to take a lead 

The leaders of the major AI organizations appear to be in agreement. 

There's been a multiplicity of events happening simultaneously that may be causing the light bulb to go on in these people's heads. 

Once again, computer programmers are taking the lead in a situation or starting something that other people had never thought about. Things like email systems, revision control systems, highly collaborative work, and bringing your dogs to work all have come out of programming environments. 

Starting with the first point, government leaders elected and appointed really are not computer programmers, nor AI people, nor technical people, so they obviously have a great deal of difficulty considering how to monitor, manage, and control computer systems. 

Second point. On one side, there have been instances of AI systems doing bad things from multiple producers. There was the OpenAI hugging face incident. There've been issues with Claude having multiple agents. These tend to be aspects of doing bad things. 

At the same time, there has been the instance of the proof of the Navier-Stokes Millennium Problem. In essence, this is a really good thing. But at the same time, it is really scaring mathematicians. Why ever learn to do math if AI can do it better than you? 

And at the same time, there's so much AI slop of incredible quality that adds another aspect that's scary. 

So it's not just one scary thing. It's multiple scary things popping up all over the place because of AI that is causing certain people to wake up. 

There have certainly been scary bits within the level of the troops, but what is interesting is that it is now the leaders of the major AI organizations that may be in agreement. The CEOs of OpenAI, Anthropic, Grok, and Meta are all concerned about what's going on and seem to be talking to each other, informing each other about the various situations. And this is based on the feedback coming from their own team members. 

It's a collective, collaborative effort amongst scientists, engineers, programmers, and mid-levels in a number of organizations. And I think I'm not talking about the HR people or the marketing people or the salespeople. I think I'm talking about the programmers, the people actually doing the hard thinking on developing AI. 

The end result is that you have a large group of well-informed people, their leaders, and a huge amount of technical knowledge and acumen, all thinking on the same wavelength about how to control, collaborate, and engage with what they are doing (in a way of being mindful of the needs of others). 

Well, this is a very good thing. Some of it depends on how Chinese developers will work into this. Given what I've seen about so much happening on GitHub and so much being open source, I can only hope and believe that Chinese programmers will come into any projects like this with engagement and enthusiasm. 

Me being me, the optimistic techno solutionist, I'm looking at all this in an optimistic and positive way. 

And I have even more of a hope. I am thinking of Linus Torvalds, who both developed Linux and Git. Usable and useful techno solutions to make people work and collaborate better Perhaps we will have another Linus who can help us write software that is Aware of what it is doing And its possible negative consequences 

At the same time, we must have something that is aware of the plight of the mathematician. Why do math if AI can always do it better than you? 

Could AI, mathematicians, and philosophers invent a new super math? Ways of thinking that take the way our brain thinks, as well as the way that computers think, and mix it together into some new way of imagining And collaborating on things?
