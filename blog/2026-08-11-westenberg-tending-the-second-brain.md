## Rule 1: Never Automate Ingestion

Choose everything you read manually.
Save only what you can articulate why you're saving it.

## Rule 2: Organize Around Problems, Not Subjects

Westenberg says follow:

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Craft_of_Research

I say follow Chris Alexander's A Pattern Language: Organise around patterns.

Focus on questions or highly specific time-bound projects.

## Rule 3: Save Notes That Disagree With You

Retain tension

## Rule 4: Delete, Don’t Just Add

Prune, Review, merge, delete

## Rule 5: Never Review Without Producing Something

Never finish a review without producing something.

I think Joan just punched me in my nasal septum. I love me some data hoarding. But of course I can't find anything. For a couple of years, I've been trying to use the same thirteen categories that Wikipedia uses And the ensuing gazillion subcategories. The problem, of course, is that any thought can often belong in multiple categories or multiple tags. Finding the right tags and the right categories can take a long time.

On the other hand, my brain is as lumpy as anybody else's brain. I probably don't have more than a few hundred typical thought patterns.

And so when I thought of the word "pattern", I immediately think of Christopher Alexander, and his book A Pattern Language, and the idea of organizing around patterns.

* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Pattern_Language
* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattern_language

Will I now start hearing the pitter patter of pretty prickly patterns wandering through my garden of mal-tended thoughts?

This post is definitely a keeper because it offers a new way of doing a triage on my hoard of stuff. So, what overarching question in my brain is Joan’s post responding to?

* How should I organize my horde of thoughts?
* How should I organize my court of thoughts that are mine and other people's?
* How can I organize my thoughts that they are both questions and patterns?
* How can I organize my thoughts into patterns of questions?
