# 2026-09-14 Two Chairs

Imagine one of the most beautiful rooms in the world. What would the room be like? It would be like a library with three or four bookshelves full of beautiful, wonderful books, including the latest books and first editions. At one end of the room, there might be a fountain surrounded by tropical plants. A large door would lead to the terrace with a view of the garden. Also, facing the garden would be a large wooden desk — big enough for stacks of papers and a pile of books so that you could use it for deep research. Next to the desk, there might be a Chinese cabinet with lots of little drawers. Each drawer would contain a toy for young people to play with. There would be a space for sitting and a table for having the afternoon tea. And of course, there would be a large marble fireplace. To each side of the fireplace would be large, comfortable chairs. These are the Two Chairs.

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Lowrie_House_(Princeton,_New_Jersey)

Each chair is but a piece of cloth and wood. A chair is not really a chair until somebody sits in it. And even then, a chair is not really something until someone is at rest. And even then, there could be something even better. And that is when the person sitting in the chair may have thoughts that help change the world. 

The thoughts that happened in the chair include the thoughts of Walter Lowrie. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Lowrie_(author) Somehow, he decided that the writings of an obscure Danish philosopher were important enough to be translated from Danish into English. Working with a small group of fellow scholars, he produced the first English translation of the works of Søren Kierkegaard. 

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B8ren_Kierkegaard

From Lowrie's translation, other translations were made. The result, for example, Kierkegaard became a very large influence on Jean-Paul Sartre, Albert Camus and other philosophers of existentialism.

I have vivid memories of visiting Uncle Walter, husband of my great-aunt Auntie B. Most of my memories involve him (when not at the dinner table), sitting in one of the chairs. He was then in his nineties He directed me and my sisters to the different drawers in the Chinese cabinet tnd tell us which toy to play with. 

After Uncle Walter died, Auntie B gave her house to Princeton University, and it is now where the president of the university lives. 
She then moved to a small house across the street from where Albert Einstein lived. And again, the chairs were in front of her fireplace, underneath the portrait of her grandmother, Barbara Allison Armour. 

Eventually, when we were living in Hong Kong, I inherited the chairs, from Auntie B. And they have been my pride and joy. 

While the chairs were in Hong Kong, Isabelle and I had them recovered with the fabric you see now. We chose a fabric that felt traditional but looked modern. 

I am thinking now these many years later, now of the fabric that was replaced. If I remember correctly, it was some kind of complicated silk fabric. This now makes me think of https://fortuny.com/history. And that makes me think of two things: we may have been just simply disposing of something that could have been historical and wonderful. At the same time, it makes me think that the chairs probably come from when Auntie B and Uncle Walter lived in Rome in the Twenties.

What is nice about the chairs is that they are large and comfortable and very practical. The frame is light. It is easy to turn the chair to face the fire or to face somebody else by pulling on the big spherical knobs at the end of the armrests.
