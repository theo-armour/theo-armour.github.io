Aha! So now we are talking about Sisyphus. 

One must assume he likes rolling his rock up the hill. 

Okay, so then we think, well, then if you do something patient, you'll find meaning in it. 

So there I am, patiently brushing my teeth twice a day, year in and year out, decade after decade, and my teeth still feel fall out.

So what is Sisyphus thinking when he's on his lunch break? 

Is he thinking about the meaning of patience? Is he just merely staring into the deep, dark, vastness of the emptiness of life itself?

The eternal dance of the sub-atomic particles. The endlessly repeating patterns 

Patience is the normal thing. The mother is breastfeeding yet another child. The farmer harvesting the grain, yet again. The lioness making another kill in his own game. 

Winning an Oscar, getting an 800 on an SAT, publishing a bestselling novel Is the chaotic exception 

So it's not really about condemning yourself to being patient. 

It's perhaps about discovering the joy of rolling the rock up the hill.

The forced patience of growing old. Meaning in a reduced pattern set 

As you age. you **must** plan to do less if you are to continue to do new, curious things, because getting through life takes more time. No longer a second or two to stand up, but perhaps dozens. many things sisyphus like. waiting for the poo. etc.
